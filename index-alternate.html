Andrew Alva

Design engineer. I build the interface and the rendering underneath it.

Ten years building for the web, five of them in real-time 3D. Trained as a graphic designer, I spent three and a half years at Active Theory shipping interactive work for Spotify, Xbox, Google, Sundance and Hulu — starting as a junior developer, finishing having owned a flagship build end to end and led the development team on another.

My work sits where typography and motion have to survive a frame budget: WebGL that coexists with accessible markup, interface polish authored as shaders, and performance that holds across a real device range.

Active Theory, 2020–2024

Playlist in a Bottle

Spotify · inaugural edition, 2023
Senior developer, end to end

Architected how a WebGL scene of interactive 3D time capsules coexists with an accessible HTML/CSS interface meeting WCAG — in-app and in-browser, across a wide device range. Built the gesture and sound interactions with the design lead and audio studio Plan8, and tuned per-device level of detail so the 3D degrades gracefully on weaker hardware without breaking either the interaction or the accessibility layer.

Breakfastverse

Reese's Puffs × AMBUSH, 2022
Development lead

Led the development team and brought junior developers and 3D artists up to speed on real-time 3D, shader authoring and the studio's in-house framework — showing an artist what a shader does to a texture or a mesh turned out to be the fastest way to make the whole team quicker. Built 3D scenes and interface with the design team, plus the gamified layer: collectible objects, scoring and a timer wired to the 2D UI through state management.

Cardano Summit

IOHK, 2021 · with Dreamwave
Core team — real-time data surfaces

Seven explorable worlds for a live two-day conference that drew more than 140,000 attendees. I built the character-customisation interfaces in plain HTML/CSS, and the WebGL signage and information screens inside the 3D world — fed by a mix of CMS content and state management so they updated in real time while the event ran, and holding up on VR headsets as well as desktop and mobile.

New Frontier

Sundance Film Festival, 2021
Core team — my first WebGL build

A WebXR festival that had to run identically on desktop, mobile and standalone Oculus Quest. I built the 2D overlay interface and several interactive components inside the 3D world: CMS-fed schedule screens, the room-access UI, and a screen that switched between an image carousel and a trailer depending on how close the visitor stood. Reading distance and transitioning smoothly across three very different device classes taught me most of what I know about cross-platform WebGL.

Made by Her: Monuments

Hulu
Core team — one system across two realities

The brief needed a normal desktop and mobile site and a VR experience. Rather than build two things, we structured it as a single 3D-environment interface with reusable components. I owned the About section and the interactive hotspots inside each monument: on desktop and mobile a hotspot triggers a camera move and a close-up with information; in VR the visitor physically walks to it. Same components, two very different interaction models.

All-RapCaviar

Spotify, 2023 & 2024 editions
Core team — engineering

Mobile-only, so the entire feel came from touch and drag: I built the animation and transitions as WebGL shaders to keep them smooth under gesture, plus the live leaderboard and a share card generated from each user's votes. The 2024 edition called for some elements to animate at a deliberately choppy low frame rate while everything else held 60fps in the same scene — faking that convincingly, without actually dropping frames and consistently across devices, is the problem I most enjoy talking about.
